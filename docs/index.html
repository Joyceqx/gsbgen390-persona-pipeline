0. Research Objective

The thesis question

Which survey-collectible feature categories most predict LLM-persona fidelity, and on which outcome dimensions?

Park et al. (2024) showed that LLM personas built from 2-hour interviews can predict a specific real person's held-out responses at ~83% of two-week test-retest reliability on GSS attitude items. Park's v2 paper also reports — but does not analyze — that the interview-vs-survey gap differs sharply by outcome: 0 on GSS attitudes, 0.15 on BFI personality, 0.28 on behavioral economic games.

The thesis fills the question Park's v2 raises but does not answer: which feature categories close which parts of those gaps on which outcomes?

The headline artifact

A 4 (feature category) × 3 (outcome dimension) feature-importance matrix. Filled by Phase 1 (GSS public-data, N≈1,500) + Phase 2 (Prolific N=20-30 with modular interviews). Park v2 implies this matrix but does not produce it.

Why this matters

Synthetic-respondent platforms — Simile (Stanford spinout, $100M raised), Aaru ($1B valuation), Synthetic Users, Evidenza, Listen Labs — are betting on which input data drives persona fidelity. No published work tells them which feature categories actually do the work, on which outcome dimensions. The thesis fills that gap with a pre-registered, OSF-locked LOO ablation across two phases that span the full 4×3 matrix in a single semester at ~$2,000 total.

Roadmap: Pilot ✅ (this dashboard) → Phase 1 GSS public data (1-4 weeks, ~$400) → Phase 2 interview-decomposed Prolific study (7-9 weeks, ~$1,600) → 4×3 matrix + writeup

1. Background — Park et al. (2024)

arXiv:2411.10109 · v1: Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People · v2 (current): LLM Agents Grounded in Self-Reports Enable General-Purpose Simulation of Individuals. Foundational paper for Stanford spinout Simile.

What Park did

  1. Recruited 1,052 stratified U.S. adults via market-research panel (Bovitz Inc.).
  2. Conducted ~2-hour AVP-protocol interviews using a custom voice-to-voice AI moderator (not human) with adaptive follow-ups; avg 6,491 words/transcript.
  3. Constructed an LLM persona for each respondent by feeding the verbatim transcript into GPT-4o as a system prompt — no fine-tuning, in-context only.
  4. Asked each persona to answer the same held-out battery the real respondent had answered: GSS attitudes, BFI-44 personality, 5 behavioral economic games, 5 social-science experiments.
  5. Compared persona-predicted vs. self-reported responses, normalized against participants' own 2-week test-retest reliability.

v1 → v2 — key reframing this sprint surfaced

The proposal cited v1's "85%" headline. v2 reorganized conditions and added per-outcome breakdowns that change the interpretation. Both versions live at the same arXiv ID; v2 is the current canonical citation.

6. Robustness — does Condition C win because of leakage?

The pilot's eval is administered in the same Cookiy session as the interview, not 2 weeks later as in Park's protocol. So some eval items have their answer effectively pre-stated in the interview content fed to the persona prompt — e.g., P1 disclosed political ideology in the open-interview portion, then minutes later was asked to self-place on the 1–7 liberal–conservative scale. So we expect Condition C (interview-conditioned) to look artificially strong.

Audit method: for each respondent × each of 15 eval items, manually tag as STRONG (construct directly stated or moderator paraphrased it), SOFT (semantically related), or CLEAN (no detectable mention). Then re-score each condition under three filters: keep all 15 items, drop STRONG only (strict-clean), or drop STRONG + SOFT (broad-clean). If C's win comes from leakage, it should collapse on the strict-clean / broad-clean columns.

Finding: C's interview-conditioning advantage survives leakage filtering. Even after dropping the 3 STRONG-leaked items per respondent (bfi_c, polviews, happy/satjob), Condition C still substantially outperforms A and B. The persona prompt apparently encodes enough personality structure that the LLM predicts uncovered items by inference, not regex.
Caveat: broad-clean has very few items left (P2 down to 4). MAE = 0.00 in those cells should be read as "consistent with no error at small N", not "perfect prediction." The honest headline is the strict-clean column.

7. Pilot limitations (each is addressed by Phase 1 or Phase 2)

In-session priming

Park separated interview from eval by 2 weeks. We did them in one 15-min session — likely inflates absolute accuracy. Resolved in Phase 2 (2-week separation).

No test-retest baseline

Park's 83% is % of test-retest reliability. We have no retest data. Resolved in Phase 1 (within-person panel structure provides the denominator).

BFI-10 instead of BFI-44

2 items per trait makes BFI trait scores statistically noisy. Resolved in Phase 2 (full BFI-44 in Wave 2).

Cookiy 15-min cap

~10% of Park's 2-hour AVP. Resolved in Phase 2 (Prolific + self-hosted AI moderator on OpenAI Realtime API, no time cap).

Panel respondent quality

One respondent gave one-word answers and asked "which AI are you?" mid-eval. Real-world deployment realism. Phase 2 adds attention checks + Prolific's vetted pool.

N=2 + N=1

Pilot is feasibility-only. No statistical inference. Phase 1 brings N≈1,500; Phase 2 brings N=20-30 with bootstrap CIs.

8. Phase 1 — GSS Public-Data Feature-Importance Analysis

Budget: ~$300-500 Timeline: 1-4 weeks N ≈ 1,500 Covers: GSS-attitudes outcome row

Research question

Within the GSS-attitudes outcome dimension — the row of Park's outcome matrix where surveys ≈ interview (0.82 vs 0.83) — which of four survey-collectible feature categories (demographic / behavioral / psychological / attitudinal) most contribute to LLM-persona predictive fidelity?

Why GSS public data first (the ROI argument)

DimensionCookiy collection (pilot)GSS public data (Phase 1)
N3 (pilot) → 30+ (thesis)1,500+ (panel) or 70,000+ (cumulative)
Cost per respondent~$10 platform + ~$1 LLM$0 platform + ~$0.10 LLM
Time to data-in-handdays to weeksminutes (already collected)
In-session primingyes (open methodological liability)none
Test-retest baselinenot availableavailable via wave-1↔wave-3 panel

Data source

  • Primary: GSS Three-Wave Panel 2010-2014 (NORC), N≈2,000 in wave 1, ~1,500 surviving wave 3 after attrition. Same respondents answered ~70 items per wave with substantial item overlap → built-in within-person test-retest.
  • Backup: GSS 1972-2022 Cumulative Cross-Section (N≈72,000) for items not covered by the panel.
  • Both freely available at gss.norc.org (registration required, no fee).

Method (Park's framework, adapted)

  1. Split features into four pre-registered categories (full mapping in gss_feature_taxonomy.json):
    • Demographic (~8 items): age, sex, race, region, education, income, marital status, employment
    • Behavioral (~10 items): voting, religious attendance, prayer, news consumption, hours worked
    • Psychological (~6 items, GSS-thin): happiness, life satisfaction, anomia subscale
    • Attitudinal (~30 items): polviews, abortion battery, racial attitudes, gender role, institutional trust
  2. Construct persona prompt from wave-1 items in the included feature categories.
  3. Predict held-out wave-3 responses via LLM persona (GPT-4o, temp 0.7, N=2 samples — same pipeline as pilot).
  4. Score: Likert MAE, % within ±1, categorical exact-match.
  5. Normalize against test-retest baseline: within-person wave-1↔wave-3 agreement = denominator. Final headline = persona accuracy ÷ test-retest reliability — directly comparable to Park's 0.82-0.83 normalized accuracy.

LOO ablation

5 conditions per respondent: full (all 4 bins) + 4 LOO (drop demographic / drop behavioral / drop psychological / drop attitudinal). With N≈1,500, every category-importance estimate gets a real bootstrap 95% confidence interval.

What Phase 1 produces

  • Confidence-intervaled feature-importance ranking on the GSS-attitudes outcome
  • First direct comparison of normalized accuracy to Park's 0.82-0.83 at comparable N
  • Pre-registered feature taxonomy → standardized vocabulary for the rest of the thesis
  • Reusable codebase that runs against any panel survey dataset

Phase 1 limitations

  • GSS-attitudes outcome only — Phase 2's job to cover BFI and games rows
  • 2-year retest interval is longer than Park's 2-week (conservative bias on the denominator — our normalized accuracy reads as a lower bound)
  • GSS panel attrition is non-random; apply panel weights or report sensitivity bounds
  • GSS is thin on psychological items (no BFI), strong on attitudinal — psychological-bin estimates will be weak

9. Phase 2 — Interview-Decomposed Feature-Importance Study

Budget: ~$1,500-1,750 Timeline: 7-9 weeks N = 20-30 Covers: BFI + games + GSS rows

Research question

Within an LLM persona built from a long interview transcript, which content categories of that interview most predict held-out responses on which outcome dimensions?

This is the non-attitudinal half of the thesis matrix — the rows where Park v2 found interview > surveys by 0.15 (BFI-44) and 0.28 (games). Park's gaps are reported as aggregate differences between "interview" and "surveys" persona conditions. Phase 2 asks which interview-content category carries the predictive signal that closes those gaps — i.e., decomposes Park's monolithic "interview" condition into pre-registered content bins.

Why this design (vs. paired-survey alternative)

AlternativeWhy it doesn't answer the question
GSS public data onlyNo interviews, no BFI, no games. Cannot observe BFI/games gap.
Paired structured survey at higher NTests "which survey features close the gap" — but Park's gap is defined as interview-vs-surveys. Surveys-only ablation cannot explain why interviews win.
Scaling pilot's 9-min Cookiy interview9 minutes is too thin to support meaningful within-transcript ablation. Depth-domain coverage already audited as inadequate at that length.

Phase 2 is the unique research move that directly decomposes Park's "interview-only" condition.

Recruitment & platform pivot

N = 20-30 panel respondents via Prolific (not Cookiy). Reasons:

  • Cookiy caps interviews at 15 minutes — incompatible with 30-45 min modular interview
  • Cookiy panel cannot be re-contacted across studies → no Wave-1 / Wave-2 separation
  • Prolific supports custom-script studies with same-respondent re-invitation across waves; ID-traceable vetted pool

Moderator: self-hosted AI agent built on OpenAI Realtime API. The pilot's Cookiy moderator script is the seed — port to a stand-alone AI agent that the team controls. The deployment recipe itself becomes a methodological contribution ("Park-comparable AI-moderated long interview at academic budget").

Wave 1 — Modular interview (30-45 min)

The interview is structured as four content modules each mapped 1:1 to a feature category. The modular structure is what enables clean transcript decomposition for the LOO ablation.

ModuleTimeFeature categoryContent
M1 — Life basics5 minDemographicAge, family, work, education, region, income — stated facts
M2 — Daily behaviors10 minBehavioralWeekly routine, consumption, exercise, social, media, voting
M3 — Inner self10 minPsychologicalChildhood, relationships, stressors, motivations, regrets, peak/valley moments
M4 — Values & attitudes10 minAttitudinalPolitics, social/policy, life philosophy, institutional trust, traditionalism

2-week separation

Park's protocol. Resolves the pilot's largest methodological liability (in-session priming).

Wave 2 — Outcome battery (~25 min)

  • BFI-44 (44 items, 1-5 Likert) → BFI personality outcome row
  • 5 behavioral-game survey vignettes (dictator, ultimatum, trust, public goods, donation) → games outcome row
  • 15 GSS attitudinal items → cross-validates Phase 1's GSS-row finding
  • 3 attention/manipulation checks → quality control

Transcript decomposition (LLM-assisted, human-validated)

  1. Stage A — module-boundary segmentation: cut transcript at moderator's verbal markers ("OK, let's move to the next part…"). First-pass clean: ~95% of content lands in the correct bin.
  2. Stage B — cross-content reassignment: pass each utterance through GPT-4o classifier prompt to detect cross-module content (e.g., M3 stress story spilling into M2 exercise). Reassign as needed.
  3. Quality control: 10% sample human-coded; agreement rate between LLM coder and human coder reported. With N=30 and ~30-min transcripts (~4000 words each), full human coding is feasible if needed (~10 min/transcript).

Output: 4 cleaned content tracks per respondent (one per module), plus a "shared/cross" residual track that goes into all LOO conditions.

LOO ablation — at the interview-content level (the structural innovation)

This is the structural innovation. The LOO doesn't drop survey items — it drops chunks of the interview transcript itself:

Why N=20-30 is enough

  1. Park's gaps are large effects (0.15, 0.28 on outcomes scaled 0-1). Detecting LOO effects of similar magnitude does not require Park's N=1052.
  2. BFI is item-rich: 30 respondents × 44 items = 1,320 paired observations per condition.
  3. Phase 2 is confirmatory after Phase 1 — Phase 1's results give priors on which categories matter most on attitudes; Phase 2 tests whether those priors hold cross-outcome. Confirmatory designs need less power than exploratory.

What Phase 2 produces

  • Headline: the first published feature × outcome 2D feature-importance map for LLM personas — covering the rows where Park identified the largest interview-vs-surveys gap (BFI 0.15, games 0.28).
  • Methodological: modular interview design as a tool for content-level ablation; LLM-assisted transcript decomposition protocol with human-coded validation; "Park-comparable AI-moderated long interview at academic budget" deployment recipe.
  • Theoretical: mechanism for Park v2's BFI/games gap (which interview content does the work); cross-outcome transferability of feature-category importance; direct response to Bisbee/Hullman "synthetic-sample sloppiness" critique via cross-outcome paired-data design.
