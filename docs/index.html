0. What is this project?

The big question

Can an LLM faithfully simulate a specific real person's responses on a held-out battery of survey questions? Park et al. (2024) showed yes — at 83% of test-retest reliability — using 2-hour deep interviews with 1,052 real people.

This pilot reproduces the architecture at small scale (N=2 interview + N=1 survey) and adds a feature-importance ablation Park did not run, designed to seed the thesis-stage replication.

Highlight: surveys-only (82%) ≈ interview-only (83%). Surveys are the cheaper, more scalable methodology — the central motivation of this thesis.

2. The data

Cookiy sessions

  • Study 1 R1: 12.5 min, 22yo male college student
  • Study 1 R2: 13.6 min, 38yo woman, AI search-quality rater
  • Study 2 R1: 9.8 min, 18-24 male, Midwest, high school

Eval battery (15 items)

  • BFI-10 (Big Five personality)
  • 4 GSS items: happy, trust, polviews, satjob
  • 1 consumer item: brand loyalty

Construction battery (18, Study 2 only)

  • 5 demographic (age, gender, education, income, region)
  • 5 behavioral (work hrs, exercise, social media, voting, religion)
  • 4 psychological (risk, planning, optimism, decision style)
  • 4 attitudinal (priority, tradition, individualism, institutional trust)
Truth table: 15 items × 3 respondents = 45 ground-truth datapoints, parsed at 100% from transcripts.

4.5 Robustness check: does Condition C win because of leakage?

The pilot's eval is administered in the same Cookiy session as the interview, not 2 weeks later as in Park's protocol. This means some eval items have their answer effectively pre-stated in the interview content fed to the persona prompt — e.g., P1 disclosed political ideology in the open-interview portion, then minutes later was asked to self-place on the 1–7 liberal–conservative scale. So we expect Condition C (interview-conditioned) to look artificially strong.

Audit method: for each respondent × each of 15 eval items, manually tag as STRONG (construct directly stated or moderator paraphrased it), SOFT (semantically related), or CLEAN (no detectable mention). Then re-score each condition under three filters: keep all 15 items, drop STRONG only (strict-clean), or drop STRONG and SOFT (broad-clean). If C's win comes from leakage, it should collapse on the strict-clean / broad-clean columns.

Finding: C's interview-conditioning advantage survives leakage filtering. Even after dropping the 3 STRONG-leaked items per respondent (bfi_c, polviews, happy/satjob), Condition C still substantially outperforms A and B. The persona prompt apparently encodes enough personality structure that the LLM predicts uncovered items by inference, not regex.
Caveat: broad-clean has very few items left (P2 down to 4). MAE = 0.00 in those cells should be read as "consistent with no error at small N", not "perfect prediction." The honest headline is the strict-clean column.

Tag rationale and evidence quotes are kept locally in leakage_audit.json (not committed to this repo because the quotes contain identifying transcript content).

5. Feature-importance: leave-one-out ablation

For Study 2's full-survey persona (Condition D), drop one feature category at a time. Larger Δ MAE means that category, when removed, hurt accuracy more — i.e., it was more predictive.

N=1 caveat: with one Study 2 respondent, each Δ MAE is a single point estimate. Treat as directional signal, not as causal evidence. The LOO design itself is the methodological contribution; the thesis-stage replication will reproduce it at N≥30 with confidence intervals.

7. Limitations (Park deviations and pilot caveats)

In-session priming

Park separated interview from eval by 2 weeks. We do them in one 15-min session. Likely inflates absolute accuracy. Acceptable as v1; thesis must adopt 2-week separation or constrain probes to disjoint constructs.

No test-retest baseline

Park's 83% is % of test-retest reliability. We have no retest data, so our raw % cannot be cited side-by-side with Park's 83%. Decide: collect retest at thesis stage, or use only relative comparisons.

BFI-10 instead of BFI-44

2 items per trait makes BFI trait scores statistically noisy. For thesis trait-level metrics to be defensible, upgrade to BFI-44.

Cookiy interview depth

15-min cap = ~10% of Park's 2-hour AVP. This is a different measurement instrument, not a sampling difference. Expect lower Condition C accuracy than Park; not evidence against the architecture.

Panel respondent quality

One respondent gave one-word answers and asked "which AI are you?" mid-eval. Real-world deployment realism, but sets a ceiling on persona-conditioning quality.

N=2 + N=1

Pilot is feasibility-only. No statistical inference. All numbers should be read as "the pipeline works and produces interpretable values," not "X is better than Y."

8. What's next (thesis roadmap)

  1. Lock the methodology. Pre-register on OSF before scaling. Switch to BFI-44, decide on retest baseline, decide on 2-week separation.
  2. Recruit so each respondent does both arms. Each person completes a 15-min interview and the 18-item survey. Enables Park's "Combined" condition (E) — the strongest in his paper.
  3. Scale to N≥30 per arm. Put error bars on LOO contributions. Statistical inference becomes possible.
  4. Item-level LOO inside the strongest category. If the pilot shows e.g. attitudinal matters most, run leave-one-item-out on the 4–8 attitudinal items to find which specific items drive the signal.
  5. Cross-model robustness. Run on Claude Sonnet 4.6 alongside GPT-4o; report how stable feature-importance ranking is across models.
  6. Publishable contribution. Park's framework × explicit feature taxonomy × cost/fidelity analysis — a clean extension positioned for a CHI / AAAI / marketing-science venue.
